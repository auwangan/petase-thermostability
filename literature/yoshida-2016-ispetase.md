## What was discovered
A bacterium was discovered in 2016 that can fully degrade and metabolize PET plastic at moderate temperatures, using two enzymes (PETase + MHETase) to break PET down into compounds it uses as food.
## Why it matters for this project
IsPETase exists and works at 30°C (the baseline you're trying to improve)
The catalytic triad (Ser-Asp-His) tells you which residues to NOT touch (they do the chemistry — mutating them breaks the enzyme)
This gives you the starting point: wild-type IsPETase. Your mutants are variants of this protein.
## Key facts to remember
- Bacterium name:  Ideonella sakaiensis
- Where was it found: samples of sediments, soil, wastewater and activated sludge
- Enzyme name(s): PETase
- Two key enzymes in the pathway: PETase, MHETase
- Catalytic triad residues: Ser-160, Asp-206, His-237 
- Optimal temperature: 30 degree Celsius 
- What does PET get broken down to: Terephthalic acid (MHET) & TPA & BHET

## My takeaway
IsPETase's unique value isn't its activity at 30°C — it's its PET-specificity. (due to the plastic state transition from semicrystalline to amorphous)
The 30°C optimum is actually a limitation. Engineering thermostability while preserving specificity is the path to industrial PET degradation.
