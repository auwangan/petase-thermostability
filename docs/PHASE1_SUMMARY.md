# Phase 1 Summary: Computational Candidate Selection

## 1. Mutation generation
ThermoMPNN site-saturation scan over the IsPETase WT structure (`6eqe_Repair.pdb`),
producing ~5300 predicted single-mutation ΔΔG values.

## 2. Structural feature annotation
Each residue annotated via a Biopython pipeline (`compute_residue_features.py`):
relative solvent accessibility (RSA), distance to the catalytic triad (S160/D206/H237),
and a hydrophobicity score.
⚠ W257 — flagged during analysis as anomalous; reason not recorded. Check
`residue_features.csv` for what stood out (outlier RSA / hydrophobicity?) and either
state the specific anomaly or drop this note.

## 3. Consensus + hardening filter
Candidates filtered by integrating ThermoMPNN ΔΔG with the structural features, then hardened with additional restrictions — notably excluding mutations that bury a charged residue in the core. Buried-charge mutations are excluded as a conservative hedge: buried charges carry a desolvation penalty and are reliably destabilizing. Note: the precision test (V68D, I83R, I145D) found that *both* FoldX and ThermoMPNN penalize buried charges sharply — so this filter is a hedge, not a correction for a demonstrated FoldX over-stabilization bias.

## 4. Diagnostics — sign-convention correction
A diagnostic pass caught a parameter error: the ThermoMPNN ΔΔG sign had been read
backward. Correct convention is negative = stabilizing. This bug had caused Week 1's
pipeline to reject all of its top candidates; corrected here.

## 5. Selection funnel
- ~30 → 15: removed mutations where FoldX and ThermoMPNN disagreed in sign.
- 15 → 13: robustness filter.
- 13 → 7: one-substitution-per-position rule (best substitution kept per position).
- 7 single mutations → candidate variants of escalating aggressiveness, stratified by
  binding-cleft proximity (= activity risk):
  - **4-mutant (conservative):** excludes the 3 mutations closest to the cleft; lowest activity risk.
  - **5-mutant (balanced) — selected lead:** accepts one cleft-proximal mutation (Q119D, ~4 Å)
    for additional predicted stability.
  - **6-mutant (aggressive):** includes further cleft-proximal mutations; higher activity risk.
  - 7-mutant (maximal): shelved — carried the N225C free-cysteine liability.

The **5-mutant (N233K / T77I / A179V / R260F / Q119D)** was chosen as the lead — the best
stability-vs-activity-risk balance. Q119D is the highest activity-risk mutation and the
primary monitoring target in Phase 2.

## 6. Validation
Five literature mutations with experimentally established stabilizing effects were run
end-to-end as positive controls. FoldX recovered the stabilizing direction for 2 of 5,
ThermoMPNN for 2 of 5, and the two agreed on only one — N233K, the lone control with
direct wet-lab validation. Both predictors are unreliable on IsPETase folding stability,
and their errors are anti-correlated between methods. Under consensus this *worsens*
recall, not improves it: requiring both tools to agree flagged just 1 of 5 known
stabilizers. Consensus is a conservative, precision-oriented filter by design, but its
precision is untested here — every control was a true positive, so the pipeline's
false-positive rate remains unmeasured. Agreement on N233K is consistent with the
precision rationale but does not prove it; a false-positive control set is required
before selected candidates can be called likely-stabilizing. (See `PHASE1_VALIDATION.md`.)

## Honest status
- The raw consensus ΔΔG values (including the 5-mutant's −6.57 kcal/mol FoldX figure) are
  pipeline outputs, **not validated results** — FoldX is unreliable on this protein. Not
  claimable as a quantitative stability gain.
- The 5-mutant is a **candidate, not a validated design**: generated by FoldX/ThermoMPNN
  consensus, anchored on the experimentally-validated N233K, pending MD + experiment.
- Activity safety: mutations **passed geometric activity-safety screens** (RSA, triad
  distance, orientation) — *passed screens*, not *confirmed activity-safe*. The real
  activity test is Phase 2 docking + MD.
